Research positioning

Compare overlapping functions without collapsing different claims.

ARVREL belongs to the broad field of software-defined and virtual protection, but its current public claim is narrower: a transparent Windows engineering laboratory that subscribes to Sampled Values, estimates fundamental quantities, evaluates virtual protection functions, applies trust gates, and preserves reviewable evidence.

Reference challenge

Lightweight Virtual Protective Relay

Public bibliographic metadata identifies Abdulmueen Alrashide's paper Lightweight Virtual Protective Relay, presented at the 2024 IEEE Industry Applications Society Annual Meeting. The associated public dissertation abstract describes a virtualization approach that abstracts hardware and uses CPU isolation for application-based execution requiring security, speed, and reliability.

87T boundary

Transformer differential protection is not a label-only addition.

ARVREL does not currently implement 87T. A credible research implementation requires multi-stream time alignment, winding and vector-group compensation, CT ratio and polarity normalization, differential and restraint quantities, percentage-bias characteristics, zero-sequence treatment, inrush restraint or blocking, CT-saturation behavior, communication-failure policy, and paired operate/secure validation.

Accurate public statement

What ARVREL can say today.

ARVREL implements a transparent IEC 61850 SV-to-virtual-protection chain with source-visible fundamental phasor estimation, multiple feeder protection functions, trust-gated operation, and deterministic software evidence. It does not yet implement 87T or claim CPU-isolated, hard-real-time, deployment-grade virtual relay performance.
